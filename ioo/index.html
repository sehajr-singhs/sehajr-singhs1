Index of Operations · IOO

Every machine draws a shape.

IOO is a representation for machine intelligence. It compiles a machine into the physical operators it's made of, joins them where they trade energy and force, and learns the shape they trace together. It's the layer a world action model for real machines needs, and the first pieces already work.

Illustration, not data. Three coupled channels of one machine, drawn as a curve in their joint state space.

The idea, in plain words

A motor, a pump and a robot joint each run inside a narrow band of what's physically possible. Couple them together and the band gets narrower still, because energy that leaves one part has to arrive at the next. So a machine's state never fills the box of all possible sensor readings. It lives on a curved surface inside that box, and that surface is the machine.

Most machine learning reads the raw numbers in the flat box. It has to relearn the physics from scratch for every unit, it breaks when someone recalibrates a sensor, and it misses faults that only show up in how the parts relate to each other. IOO reads the surface instead.

How it works

  1. Index the operators. Every hardware primitive (DC motor, pump, thermal mass, actuator) gets its own local geometry, a physical operating envelope, and ports where it exchanges energy, mass, heat or force with its neighbors.
  2. Compose at the interfaces. Primitives join into one graph at their physical interfaces. A class of machines is just a different arrangement drawn from the same index.
  3. Read conservation, not levels. A healthy interface conserves what flows across it. IOO measures how much that conservation slips. Because the slip is normalized by magnitude, per-channel recalibration cancels exactly (checked numerically to 1e-14).
  4. Act on the shape. A message-passing model reads the graph for detection, prediction and control, and a slip gate turns adaptation on only when a boundary stops conserving. When nothing is wrong, control is untouched.

What world models throw away

Today's world models and vision-language-action policies compress what they see into a latent and treat fine-grained signal as noise. That's the right call for predicting where a ball lands. It's the wrong call for machines, because a bearing, a winding or a weld fails as a small change in how two parts relate long before anything shows up at the macro level. IOO gives every operator its own axes, so the low level stays visible. In simulation it flagged every injected fault with a median lead of about 90 seconds, while a correlation detector fired at the moment of failure.

A thought experiment

Give a robot two rounded stubs instead of hands and ask it to type. A policy trained on video of human fingers has nothing to imitate. A model that indexes its own body as operators sees a geometry problem instead. The stub's contact patch covers three keys, so it tilts until the patch covers one, feels for slip at the contact, and corrects. A new body is a new shape drawn from the same index, not a new dataset.

That's where IOO is going, not what it does today. The closest evidence so far is in control: in simulation, a controller with frozen weights carried through a swap from a DC motor to an AC motor.

A flywheel that runs on structure

Most of physical AI is betting on collecting more hours of data. IOO bets on reuse. Every machine it reads adds operators to the index, and the next machine built from those operators starts with a prior instead of an empty model. The models are graphs of tens of nodes that run on a CPU, which means they can live on the machine itself.

What it doesn't do yet

  • Control is simulation-only, on a motor-and-flywheel plant, and there's no stability certificate yet tying the slip gate to closed-loop robustness.
  • Operators are modeled by hand, and the catalog is mostly rotating machinery.
  • The property that makes the shape immune to recalibration also blinds it to slow level drift, which is real wear. It complements raw statistics rather than replacing them.
  • On 10-minute wind-farm SCADA data nothing works, IOO included, because the averaging erases the dynamics. That's the edge of where this applies.

Where it came from

Electric Adaptation asked for motor controllers that keep learning as hardware ages, built from "operators" inside a Lyapunov-stable core. The Shapes a Machine Draws showed that the relationships between a machine's channels identify it and survive re-instrumentation. IOO is what both were reaching for. My work on certified Lyapunov functions with Prof. Wenqi Cui is how the control side gets proofs.
